Introduction

Indian Railways operates one of the largest rail networks in the world. This section traces why automated protection became necessary, what came before Kavach, and the operating conditions Kavach was engineered against.

1.1History of Railway Accidents in India

Railway accident investigations have consistently shown that a majority of severe collisions originate from operational human error rather than infrastructure failure — Signal Passed At Danger (SPAD), excessive speed, miscommunication between station staff and loco pilots, and driver fatigue chief among them. These recurring causes exposed the limits of relying on visual signal observation alone.

1.2Need for Automatic Train Protection

As speeds crossed 110–130 km/h, the reaction window available to a loco pilot shrank accordingly — a human response delay of 1.5–2 seconds becomes safety-critical near a restrictive signal or speed-restricted zone. Automatic Train Protection continuously supervises movement, signal state, braking curves, and speed limits, warning the driver and intervening automatically when a limit is about to be breached.

1.4Train Protection Before Kavach

Before Kavach, Indian Railways trialled three distinct technologies — each solved part of the problem and exposed the next requirement.

🚦 Auxiliary Warning System (AWS) — Mumbai Suburban

Trackside permanent magnets and an onboard electromagnetic receiver generated an audible/visual cab warning at signals; unacknowledged warnings triggered automatic braking.

Advantages
  • Simple, low-cost, proven technology
  • Reduced SPAD incidents
Limitations
  • Point-based only, no continuous supervision
  • No collision avoidance, unsuited to high speed

📡 Anti-Collision Device (ACD) — Konkan Railway

India's first indigenous collision-avoidance system: GPS positioning plus UHF radio let trains broadcast position, speed and direction to compute collision risk and trigger braking.

Advantages
  • Indigenous, train-to-train communication
Limitations
  • GPS errors in tunnels/valleys/urban canyons
  • No SIL-4 certification, no EI integration

🚄 Train Protection & Warning System (TPWS) — ETCS L1

Eurobalises, trackside loops, and an onboard processor continuously supervise speed and braking curves, enforcing brakes on approach to a danger signal at excess speed.

Advantages
  • SIL-4 certified, internationally proven
Limitations
  • ₹50–60 lakh/km — too costly to scale nationally
Kavach exists because no single prior system offered all four properties at once: SIL-4 safety, collision avoidance, Electronic Interlocking interoperability, and a cost low enough for nationwide deployment.

Key Features of Kavach

Kavach fuses cab signalling, continuous speed supervision, SPAD prevention, collision avoidance, RFID positioning, automatic horn control, and network-wide emergency braking into one SIL-4 platform.

🖥️ Cab Signalling

Signal aspect, target speed, distance-to-target and next-signal status displayed directly in the cab.

⚡ Speed Supervision

Enforces Maximum Permissible Speed and Temporary Speed Restrictions with real-time monitoring and automatic brake enforcement.

🚦 SPAD Prevention

Detects a red-signal approach, warns the driver, monitors response, and applies automatic train stop.

🚂 Collision Prevention

Secure train-to-train and train-to-station wireless links protect against head-on and rear-end collisions.

📯 Auto Whistling

RFID tags identify level crossings and trigger the horn automatically.

🆘 Emergency SOS

A manual SOS broadcast halts every Kavach-equipped train within a 3 km radius.

03

System Architecture

Kavach splits into two cooperating subsystems: fixed trackside infrastructure that senses and broadcasts safe movement authority, and onboard locomotive electronics that enforce it in real time.

3.1Trackside (Wayside) Subsystem

3.1.1 RFID Balise — Why RFID Over GPS

GPS gives absolute position but not always to the precision SIL-4 signalling demands.

Limitations of GPS

  • 5–15 m position error
  • Blocked in tunnels
  • Multipath reflection in cities
  • Weather-related degradation
  • Not deterministic enough for SIL-4

Advantages of RFID

  • Exact fixed-track reference
  • Works in tunnels and dense fog
  • Immune to satellite loss
  • Deterministic position update

Kavach uses GPS for coarse positioning and RFID Balises for precise correction: every Balise pass recalibrates the onboard odometer.

SIMULATION — RFID BACKSCATTER ENERGY HARVESTING

The Balise carries no battery. It powers up only inside the reader's near-field, then answers by backscatter — reflecting the reader's own carrier rather than transmitting.

3.1.2 Signal Interface Unit (SIU)

Legacy relay- and lamp-based signals can't talk to a computerised ATP network. The SIU bridges that gap: it senses lamp current/voltage, isolates the field circuit, digitises the aspect, and forwards it over RS-485.

Only light crosses the opto-isolation barrier, so traction surges and lightning transients on the field side can never reach the safety processor.

3.1.3 Station Kavach Master Unit

The central intelligence node: it fuses Electronic Interlocking data, SIU inputs, track circuits, point machines, and RFID/radio data to compute Movement Authority and broadcast it over UHF.

3.1.4 Radio Towers & UHF/LTE Communication

Kavach operates in the 406–470 MHz UHF band, which propagates almost entirely by line-of-sight. Towers stand 30–45 m tall purely to raise the radio horizon over buildings, wagons, and terrain — not for coverage area alone.

SIMULATION — UHF LINE-OF-SIGHT & TDMA SLOTTING
Active TDMA slot: TRAIN 1
Link status: LOCKED — LoS clear

Each participant transmits only in its own time slot, so simultaneous trains never collide on the same frequency — collision-free, deterministic, low-latency safety messaging.

Tower height, antenna gain, and TDMA slotting together give Kavach its 5–10 km deterministic radio link.

Collinear Antenna

6–12 dBi gain, 360° coverage, narrow vertical beamwidth — omnidirectional so link quality doesn't depend on train heading.

GFSK Modulation

Gaussian-filtered FSK gives better spectral efficiency, lower bit-error rate, and strong mobile-radio performance.

Lightning Arrestor

Diverts strikes >100 MV / 30 kA to earth before they reach RF transceivers.

Dual Redundant Radios

Radio A/B run simultaneously; if one fails, the other takes over — no single point of failure.

Adjacent Station Kavach units are also linked by an Optical Fibre Cable ring, so station-to-station safety data keeps flowing even if a radio link degrades.

3.2.1 Onboard Vital Computer (OVC)

Also referred to as the TCAS processor — the central decision-making unit, classified SIL-4, running on a 2-out-of-3 hardware-voting architecture identical in principle to Station Kavach's.

  • Continuous position calculation
  • Speed supervision
  • Braking-curve generation
  • Movement-authority enforcement
  • Collision prediction
  • Driver warning generation
  • Emergency brake activation
  • Event recording
  • Communication management
  • Safety diagnostics

Processors are railway-certified (NXP PowerPC, ARM Cortex-R/A) with ECC-protected memory, lockstep CPU operation, and watchdog timers, running a Real-Time Operating System (VxWorks / Integrity / QNX / SafeRTOS) so braking calculations always pre-empt lower-priority tasks like logging.

3.2.3 RFID Reader & Antenna Configuration

Two independent reader antennas are mounted beneath the bogie, 200–600 mm above the Balise, aimed vertically downward for near-field coupling.

Antenna 1 detects tag
Antenna 2 detects same tag
Detection order → travel directionA1→A2 forward · A2→A1 reverse

The dual layout doubles as hardware redundancy: if one reader fails, the other keeps supplying position data. Filtering algorithms reject noise from 25 kV traction lines, motors and switching converters.

3.2.4 Speed and Distance Processing Unit (SDPU)

Wheel encoders (1024–4096 pulses/revolution) give fine-grained speed and distance via Δd = 2πR·(Npulses/NPPR) — but assume perfect wheel–rail adhesion, which rain, oil, and leaf contamination break.

SIMULATION — WHEEL SLIP vs DOPPLER RADAR FUSION
Wheel encoder speed: 90.0 km/h
Doppler radar speed: 90.0 km/h
Fused (trusted) speed: 90.0 km/h
As slip grows, the encoder over-reads speed while radar keeps tracking true ground velocity — the OVC trusts radar once the gap exceeds a safe threshold.

3.2.5 Brake Interface Unit (BIU)

The final safety actuator: it turns an OVC decision into physical deceleration by regulating brake-pipe air pressure through electro-pneumatic valves.

ModeTriggerBehaviour
Normal Service Brake (NSB)Station approach, speed restrictionGradual pressure reduction, smooth deceleration

Even if the driver doesn't react and communication is lost, this is the fail-safe: pipe pressure collapses, brake cylinders apply throughout the rake, and the train sheds speed to a stop.

7.1 Heavy Rain & Waterlogging

Traditional track circuits detect occupancy by passing current through the rails — water accumulation creates leakage paths that can cause false occupancy readings. RFID Balises sidestep this: sealed in IP68 enclosures, they need no batteries and keep answering the reader's field even submerged.

Failure Analysis & Risk Assessment

Railway signalling follows the fail-safe principle: every abnormal condition must resolve toward a safe state. FMEA (Failure Mode and Effects Analysis) identifies what can go wrong, its effect, and Kavach's mitigation.

RFID Reader Failure

Antenna cable damage, connector corrosion, reader fault, Balise misalignment.

Mitigation: OVC falls back to encoder + radar odometry; speed capped at 30 km/h until a valid tag is next detected.

UHF Radio Failure

Hardware fault, RF interference, antenna damage, jamming, power loss.

Mitigation: last valid Movement Authority is held; Normal Service Brake auto-applies if the link isn't restored within 5 seconds.

Wheel Encoder Failure

Cable breakage, sensor damage, misalignment, pulse loss.

Mitigation: cross-checked continuously against Doppler radar; a mismatch beyond threshold raises a fault, alerts the driver, and initiates safe braking.

OVC Processor Failure

Cosmic-radiation bit flips, memory corruption, lockup, clock failure.

Mitigation: 2oo3 voting isolates the outlier board; the remaining two continue operating safely (see simulation above).
